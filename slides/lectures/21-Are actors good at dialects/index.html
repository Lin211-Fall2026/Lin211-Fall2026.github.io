Stereotypes

  • A stereotype is a cognitive structure that includes the beliefs, preconceived knowledge, and expectations about a particular social group as held by a particular perceiver
    • It’s about abstract knowledge of what it means to belong to a particular group, to have a particular set of characteristics
    • Widely held, fixed, and oversimplified
  • Where do these things come from?

Why do we do this?

  • According to cognitive psychologists, stereotypes are a natural part of human categorization processes
    • It’s cognitively efficient: Once you have the categories, you can save on processing time by not reconsidering each “member” of a group
    • It helps us predict our social world: We don’t have to worry because you can easily apply your categories to new individuals
    • It makes us feel good about ourselves: We like our own group (ingroup) better than other groups (outgroups)
    • It’s automatic?: They can even be activated and used without you being aware of it
      • Subliminal primes are stimuli that activate stereotypes without our conscious perception (subliminal messaging!)

How do we learn stereotypes?

  • According to social learning theory, we learn our stereotypes from
    • Our parents
    • Our peer groups
    • The media

An Example: The Lion King

Good Guys Bad Guys Others

  • The Lion King , a Disney movie set in Africa, has good guys, bad guys, and others (like sidekicks)
  • How are they represented?
    • Good guys speak “standard”, bad guys don’t
    • Others have varying accents

Characters, varieties, and traits

  • Why do they do this?
    • To create an effect, help us “know” the character
      • Some of the personality is done through the animation
        • Ex. Scar is smaller than Mufasa, has a dark mane = not strong but evil
      • But you can build a better character through linguistic stereotypes!
  • Because logically…
    • Why would anyone in the African plains speak with an American or British accent anyway?
    • Why would two brothers have such differing accents?
    • Why would no characters speak an African language?
    • How can you make it palatable to an American audience?

What are the consequences when we use adult stereotypes about voices and social groups to create characters and narratives for consumption by children?

  • Disney can decide it wants to have certain voices to give life to their characters, but the extent to which this is done well is really on the actor (and their dialect coach , a person who helps actors craft a voice for characters in the context of a production)
    • Most actors are from a place and can use their native dialect to make the character come to life or the accent is less important to the story
    • Others work very hard at developing a specific accent and often appear in roles using only it
    • Others pick up new dialects for specific productions, working with a dialect coach to modify the phonetics of their speech
      • Other dialect features would be in the written script
      • Some of these are really good!
      • And some of these are really bad…
