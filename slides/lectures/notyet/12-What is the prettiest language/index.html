Perceptual Dialectology

  • Perceptual Dialectology (also known as “folk linguistics”) is the study of how nonlinguists classify different dialects, as well as their beliefs about and attitudes toward different dialects and their speakers
    • It deals with where nonlinguists believe variation comes from, where nonlinguists believe variation exists, and why nonlinguists believe variation happens
  • In many traditional dialectology studies, there is a lack of inclusion of nonlinguists’ attitudes and beliefs about linguistic varieties and variation
    • Perceptual dialectology fills this gap
    • Contrasts with perspective that only production data counts as “real” data

What are language ideologies?

  • Language ideologies are a set of beliefs, feelings, ideas, or thoughts about languages as they are used in everyday, social settings
    • Linguists and anthropologists study these in order to understand certain worldviews held by the cultures they are investigating
    • Part of a larger social/cultural system; engrained, broad, socio-cultural schemas
    • Connections between language use and reactions to language use
    • Two-way street!
  • All varieties of all languages display variation
  • Variation may be tied to various factors:
    • identity – variation may relate to social groups (i.e., age, region, ethnicity, gender, etc.)
    • context – variation may relate to the context in which language is used (i.e., register)
    • genre – variation may relate to spoken or written genres (i.e., joke, sermon, argument, etc.)
  • Variation happens in all domains of a language variety
    • Words, sounds, sentence structure, etc.
    • Conscious and unconscious…

Language as social action

Belonging

Acknowledging that you have a certain accent/dialect means you acknowledge that you belong to some group of people who have that accent/dialect (and don’t belong to another…)

It’s all about identity and belonging…

But what we know about language ideologies is that they often lead to/conjure up stereotypes…

Stereotypes!

  • Stereotypes are (over)generalizations made about a particular group of people
    • True for one = true for all!
    • Positive vs. negative? Explicit vs. implicit?
  • Why do we do it?
    • Useful in making quick decisions – see several police cars at a donut shop, choose not to go to that donut shop
    • Remember, humans seek patterns
  • Problems?
    • Encourages prejudice
  • The point is this
    • Ideas about “correct” forms of language are primarily social (rather than linguistic) and are largely based upon stereotypes about the social groups that speak particular varieties
    • The idea of what is “correct” varies from country to country, culture to culture (compare “proper” British and American English)
    • The same linguistic process may be “correct” in one society and “incorrect” in another (e.g., Word-final /r/ in varieties of English, multiple negation)
    • The variety that is considered “correct” is almost universally the variety of the social group with the most political and economic power in a given society
