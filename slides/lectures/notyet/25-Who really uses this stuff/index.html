Course description:

This course is designed to give students a broad introduction to the field of linguistics from the perspective of language users . As native users of at least one spoken or signed language, students are already in some ways experts in how language works. This course will explore the beliefs about and perceptions of language that they already possess and build upon this knowledge by comparing popular notions about language with those developed in linguistic science , paying attention to the contributions of popular belief as well as the differences between them and scientific findings.

Student Learning Outcomes

Careers in linguistics?

  • Yes, some of us become professors (though this is a vanishingly small number…)
  • Obvious jobs = ESL/language teacher, translation/interpretation, speech and hearing sciences
  • You can find people with linguistics degrees (or linguistics training) in a number of non-academic spheres, including but not limited to:
    • The not-for profit space: research, scientific writing, document design, media production, digital storytelling, advocacy
    • Tech: human-language technology, UX, taxonomy, localization/internationalization, conversational AI, voice user interface
    • Business: consulting, training/development, HR, onboarding, leadership development, intercultural communication
    • Government: Census, FBI, CIA, NSA, strategic communications

Skills breakdown: If you are a linguist, you can:

Design and execute research projects (“end-to-end research design”)

Recruit participants

Develop rapport with both individuals and groups for purposes of data collection

Interview participants & collect data

Analyze the data (qualitatively & quantitatively)

Synthesize your results

Present your findings
