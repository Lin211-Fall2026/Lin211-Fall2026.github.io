Diachronic vs. synchronic

  • More interested in diachronic (changes through time) than synchronic (changes within time)
    • Synchronic changes = sociolinguistics!
    • More on that later, but…
  • Diachronic changes always begin as synchronic patterns!
    • Once these changes pile up, new languages are born!
    • But not everything changes, and not all at once…

Language Families

  • Linguists examine languages as part of what are called language families
    • Ex. Germanic, Romance, Slavic
    • The idea is that every language descends from another language, which, in turn, descended from another language
    • Think of it like a family tree!
  • Over time, dialects can change so much that they become mutually unintelligible
    • In other words, speaker of dialect A can’t understand a speaker of dialect B
    • When this happens, the two dialects can be considered new languages unto themselves

Language relatedness

  • The idea is that, like you have received traits (e.g., hair/eye color) from your parents, so too do languages receive traits from their parent languages!
    • How do we know? Couldn’t it be borrowings or chance?
    • Look at the core vocab!
  • There are more than 100 language families, and most of the world’s languages are classified into one of them
    • Romance (pictured here) is a branch of the Proto-Indo-European tree ::: {.notes}

Signed languages are separate; as are creoles and pidgins. Some are mixed. Some are isolates/

  • A proto-language is a postulated ancestral language from which a number of languages are believed to have descended by evolution, forming a language family
    • Attested = languages we have evidence for their existence (e.g., Latin)
    • Proto-languages are usually unattested , or partially attested at best
    • They are reconstructed by way of the comparative reconstruction
  • Languages like “Proto-Indo-European” are reconstructed languages
    • That is, we have no direct record of it, but we use the daughter languages to attempt to understand what language would have been like!

Proto-Indo-European

  • Proto-Indo-European is thought to be the common ancestor in the Indo-European language family, which includes branches like Indo-Iranian, Germanic, Celtic, and Italic
    • Family = largest (46% of the world’s population speaks an Indo-European language as a first language)
    • Cool story: everyone was on board to say Greek and Latin were related. In the 1700s, a guy named William Jones wanted to add Sanskrit, an Indian language, to the mix. He had a bunch of it wrong, but it still made the connection between European languages and others more apparent.
  • For context, we believe Proto-Indo-European was spoken around 4500-2500 BCE somewhere in eastern Europe, near the Black Sea
    • Fun fact: they were nomadic and likely responsible for domesticating horses!
    • Speakers migrated away from the area and lost touch, therefore creating different groups then different dialects then different languages
    • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language
