What is a chatbot?

  • Chatbots (previously called “chatterbots”) are computer programs/applications/web interfaces that are designed to have conversations with users
    • Sometimes text, sometimes speech
    • Most today are based on generative artificial intelligence , a type of AI that produces language by using models that learn the patterns and structures of training data to produce new language forms
    • Older models (like ELIZA) were still based on AI, but not like ChatGPT, which are based on large language models , or LLMs, a type of machine learning model that uses natural language processing and that has predictive power on linguistic factors (e.g., syntax, semantics) because it is based on a human language corpus.

Corpus Linguistics

  • Corpus linguistics is a research paradigm that considers language as text in the form of a “corpus” (Lat. for “body”)
    • A corpus – like the one you have helped create in the “Data Collection” assignment – is a dataset that contains a large body of language data that are either originally text-based (like a scraped website) or digitized spoken language (like the Buckeye Corpus on the next slide)
    • A corpus is usually annotated , or marking additional information with the language data; and example would be a part-of-speech tagger that indicates the lexical category for a word in a tag that makes it easily searchable

Where does meaning come from?

  • Much of meaning in the world boils down to individual human experiences with language
    • Sometimes we’re focused on intention , what the speaker meant to convey, as opposed to some kind of hard and fast definition (e.g., when a friend says, “I’m cold” = state of being BUT ALSO perhaps “Can you get me a blanket?”)
    • Chatbots, by definition, cannot have intentions
    • Pragmatists might say, then, that their utterances have no content = meaningless
    • But humans are predisposed to assigning meaning to language!!
  • It appears that humans engage with machines AS IF the machine were producing meaningful utterances (like humans would)
    • These “counterfeit people” deceive us into thinking they are real!
    • But human speech = tied to human experience! ::: {.notes}

Stochastic Parrots?

  • Emily Bender describes LLMs as “stochastic parrots”
    • Stochastic means something is randomly determined or has a random probability distribution
    • “for haphazardly stitching together sequences of linguistic forms … according to probabilistic information about how they combine, but without any reference to meaning.” (Bender)
    • The expression means generative AI can create surprisingly real-sounding texts using statistical modelling, but, like parrots, they can’t understand what they have produced
  • The idea is that chatbots can carry on a conversation in a way that mimics a human conversational partner
    • But what is really happening?
    • Remember – computers are stupid, so any “intelligence” these AI-based systems have are from humans in some way
    • “You can’t expect a machine-learning system to learn stuff that’s not in it’s training data…otherwise you’re expecting magic.” (Bender) = if no one trained the system to understand “skibbidi” as used by native speakers of the variety, it won’t get it (until it trains on new input in the form of your talk)
    • Indeed, even the notion of “understanding” may be too much!
  • And, in reality, LLMs are really like black boxes, so we often have no idea what’s going on behind the scenes
