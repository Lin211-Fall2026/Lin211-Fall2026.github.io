Language exhibits arbitrariness

…has a grammar

…exhibits duality of patterning

  • Duality of patterning is a feature of language whereby discrete units of language at one level (such as the level of sounds) can be combined to create different kinds of units at a different level (such as words)
    • That is, we think of language as having two distinct levels that we encounter at the same time
    • Meaningless/arbitrary elements like sounds AND
    • Meaningful elements like words
    • A limited set of meaningless things that we can put together to make essentially infinite meaningful things!

Do animals have “Language”?

  • We find that in nearly all cases, systems of non-human communication:
    • are non-arbitrary;
    • not culturally transmitted;
    • do not exhibit duality of patterning;
    • do not change;
    • lack creativity;
    • do not allow the animal to discuss past, future or hypothetical events, and most importantly
    • DO NOT __ __ have grammar!

But can any animals learn Language?

Perhaps a better comparison to human language may be found in studies of primates (mostly chimps), who are closely related to humans

The problem?

Chimpanzees do not have the same mouth and throat anatomy as humans, which makes speaking an impossible task

With a tremendous amount of effort, Viki (one chimpanzee in early work on this question) learned to (sort of) pronounce a few words like papa and cup

  • The more recent work with bonobos has brought the entire research to a different focal question – when apes use the signs we teach them, do they know what it means?
    • That is, they might use the signs in a way that looks like how humans do, but do they perceive it in the same way or are they just mimicking?
    • Savage-Rumbaugh suggests that in the earlier experiments, apes were NOT using language symbolically
    • Instead, it was conditioned behavior (do X, get Y)
    • She thinks we have to make this step first (teaching symbolism) before we can find out whether they can learn language

Birds: Calls vs. Songs

  • Are typically not done in winter
  • Are primarily restricted to males
  • Are unique by species, can even vary by individual
  • Purpose of songs?
    • To announce and delimit the territory of the male
    • To attract a mate
  • Are made year round
  • Can be heard in both males and females
  • For many birds, individual calls associated with specific activities
  • Purpose of calls?
    • To warn of predators
    • To coordinate flocking and flight activity
    • To express aggression
    • To accompany nesting or feeding behavior.

Chaffinches

Many bird species do not develop fully characteristic songs if they’re not exposed to them early in life

If chaffinches are reared in isolation, they do sing, but only the the typical song of the species.

If young chaffinches are reared away from adults, but with other young chaffinches, the entire experimental community develops an identical song .
