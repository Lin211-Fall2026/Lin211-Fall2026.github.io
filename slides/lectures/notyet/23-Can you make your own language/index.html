Create your own?

  • One of the key things we have said about language is that it is acquired , which suggests something that happens to a human naturally
  • Languages that are used in the “real world” are called natural languages (sometimes natlang for short)
  • But, in “fictional world(s)”, we may run across constructed languages (or conlangs for short)
    • Conlangs are any language that has been consciously created
    • These might be like Dothraki from Game of Thrones or various languages in the Star Wars and Marvel franchises, for example
    • We can also call these kind of languages fictional languages
    • Not all conlangs are just in fictional worlds though – languages like Esperanto exist

Why would we make our own?

  • Aside from the Hollywood angle, one might make another language to:
    • Help make communication easier (like making what’s called an international auxiliary language , which can be used by people who speak other languages when they need to communicate; kind of like a lingua franca ) = Esperanto
    • Experiment with what is possible within the areas of linguistics, cognitive science, and machine learning
    • Make a game! As a hobby!
  • As we’ll see, they require a lot of work, so very few gain a lot of traction beyond their original creation reason
    • Esperanto has gained a bit of ground over time, but no other planned languages have really caught on
    • With the advent of the internet, fans have been more particular about having “good” languages in their films and shows (and will drag producers online if they don’t)
  • Think about our “bits and pieces” we’ve been discussing
    • Sounds
    • Rules about sounds
    • Morphemes
    • Rules about morphemes
    • Sentence structure
    • Rules about sentence structure
    • Pragmatics
    • Writing system (maybe)
    • Etc., etc., etc.

What do you need?

  • Here is just a small list of things you would need to decide about your language:
    • Which consonants/vowels?
    • How do you make syllables?
    • What are the rules about putting sounds together?
    • Is your language stress-based or tonal?
    • How would someone know you were asking a question?
    • What parts of speech?
    • What are the words?
    • How do you mark nouns for number/gender/role?
    • Do adjectives agree?
    • Does it have articles like “the” and “a”?
    • How do you conjugate verbs?
    • Do you mark tense/aspect/mood?
    • What is your language’s word order?

To be a language…

  • You need a self-contained sound system that is distinct from other languages
    • This is why “Pig Latin” isn’t really a different language
  • You need a self-contained word/sentence system that is distinct from other languages
    • Also why “Pig Latin” isn’t a different language
  • It must be systematic…
    • Ubese ( Star Wars )
    • Yaté __ __ yaté __ __ yotó .
    • I have come for the bounty on this Wookie.
    • Yotó __ __ yotó .
    • 50,000, no less.

And…

  • Your language will inevitably be embedded in some kind of culture
    • When a character in a movie uses another language, it is because that person is being represented as belonging to that culture
    • Like with our discussion of movies, it is important to the character development
    • And it shouldn’t be random! There will be aspects of your culture that matter to your language
      • Ex. If your culture is very focused on politeness, your language will need to have built-in ways to show deference, kind of like Japanese does
      • Ex. If you want to represent a hierarchical society, you will have standard/proper dialects spoken by those in higher positions and non-standard versions spoken by people in lower positions

Writing system

  • We already talked about the fact that
    • Not all languages are written
    • Writing is learned, not acquired
    • There are many ways to represent language in writing
    • Writing must represent language (not pictograms)
  • What does this mean for a conlang?
    • You can use a pre-existing writing system!
    • You can make it up altogether!
    • Remember – the squiggles we make to represent English are just as arbitrary as any other way to represent language!
    • Must be systematic!
