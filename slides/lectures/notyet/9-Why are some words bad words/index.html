Why do we use “bad words”?

  • It’s usually about emotion!
    • Neurological, psychological, and sociocultural
    • A learned behavior
  • Some people talk about it with respect to politeness
    • That’s where pragmatics comes back!
    • You should know, however, that swearing is not universally rude
    • It can be funny, cathartic, automatic, related to brain injury, etc.

Doing stuff with language

  • Language is the principal means we have to “do stuff”
    • To greet, compliment, and insult one another,
    • To plead or flirt,
    • To seek and supply information, and
    • To accomplish hundreds of other daily tasks
  • Actions that are carried out through language are called speech acts
    • Such as lying, promising, greeting, etc.
    • Let’s focus on indirect speech acts

Politeness

  • It’s all about “saving face”
  • Brown and Levinson (1987) discussed the most influential and comprehensive model for understanding politeness – face-saving model
    • Face = one’s public self-image
      • Universal notion in human societies
      • Two kinds of face:
        • Positive face: one’s desire to be liked (See positive politeness…)
        • Negative face: one’s desire to be left alone (See negative politeness…)

Why do we do that?

Think about who you feel comfortable swearing around

Remember that it’s a learned behavior

If swearing is seen as rude by the hearer, the speaker has violated the hearer’s negative face (to be left alone)

Self-censorship (to save others’ faces) potentially threatens the speaker’s positive face (perhaps swearing is their most charming feature)

And even if broad social norms make swearing more acceptable in some contexts, because of their connection to rudeness, etc., some listeners may find it unwelcome
