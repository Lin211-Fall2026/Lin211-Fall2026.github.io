Quick Review

  • All languages have words, but what is a word?
    • Not really about letters and spaces…
    • It’s all about meaning!
  • Knowing a word requires:
    • Phonetic/phonological knowledge
    • Syntactic knowledge
    • Morphological knowledge
    • Semantic knowledge
  • We introduced:
    • Phonetics is the study of the sounds in a language
    • Phonology is the study of the system of contrastive relationships among the speech sounds
    • Syntax is the study of the way in which words are put together to form phrases and sentences
    • Morphology is the study of word formation, including a word’s component parts, internal structure, and rules for word creation
    • Semantics is the study of meaning

Morphemes?

  • What does it mean that we use morphological rules to create new words?
    • Like “happy” becoming “unhappy” by adding the un- prefix, people invent new words all the time by simply combining materials (morphemes!) already in the language
    • For example, “Instagram” is a portmanteau (or a kind of blending) of the words “instant camera” and “telegram” – two old technologies that get repurposed for the photo-sharing app
      • The creators took morphemes from those two words (sort of…”insta-” is weird) and smushed them together
      • Lots of examples of portmanteaus – like?
    • But it is also how we convert the proper name “Instagram” into new kinds of words!
      • Can you make “Instagram” into an adjective?
      • Make it fit this sentence – “That picture is so ____!” or “That is an ____ picture!”
